# Impeccable

Design guidance for this repo. Gathered by `/impeccable:teach-impeccable`.

## Design Context

### Users

The public tier (`jomcgi.dev`) is built for a **technical audience evaluating the
work on its merits**: peers, collaborators, and anyone professionally assessing
engineering depth.

That audience shapes every decision. They arrive skimming, they leave in under a
minute if nothing catches, and they judge competence from the artifact itself
rather than from a claim about it. The job to be done is: *make the depth legible
fast, without requiring the visitor to read code.* A working demo beats a
description of a demo. A measured number (`~25 ms request to warm semgrep scan`)
beats an adjective. Craft matters because sloppiness reads as a signal about the
engineering behind it.

Two reading modes to serve, in priority order:

1. **The skimmer**, who has a minute and wants to know whether there is substance
   here. Optimize for this one. Headline, working demo, and a real number carry
   the weight.
2. **The depth reader**, who will actually open an `ARCHITECTURE.md` or an STPA model. Reward
   them with density and precision once they commit, but never at the cost of the
   skimmer's first minute.

A third group arrives for one specific tool (the ships map, the hike finder).
For them the design should get out of the way entirely and let the tool work.

### Brand Personality

**Playful, rigorous, fast.** Three words that pull against each other on purpose,
held together by one governing rule:

> Less but better. It can look nice, but ultimately function is beautiful.

That is the tiebreaker whenever the three pull apart. Decoration that does not
carry information loses. An animation that does not communicate state loses. The
sheet's energy comes from density and precision: flat fills, 1px rules and
meaningful colour only, never gradient meshes and blur.

- **Playful, characterful.** Willing to be memorable and slightly irreverent,
  in the copy and in what the tools do: a trail that says where you are, a
  record numbered like a parts list, a 404 that names the path you tried.
  Never at the expense of clarity.
- **Rigorous, no fluff.** Confidence through evident care. Claims are backed by
  real numbers from real hardware. Nothing decorative that is not earning its
  place.
- **Fast and utilitarian.** Speed is part of the aesthetic. Chrome is minimal.
  The tools work instantly or they have failed.

Anti-pattern to avoid: **generic SaaS**. Soft blurred shadows, a purple gradient
hero, rounded pill everything, a stock illustration of people collaborating. The
whole visual language is a deliberate rejection of that, and drifting back toward
it is the main risk in any redesign.

Second anti-pattern, recorded from real feedback: **washed-out muted greys**.
Grey-on-beige secondary text has been vetoed twice on this codebase (once on the
homepage, once on the ember mini-site). Secondary text stays secondary through
size and weight, never through washout. See `--em-muted` in
`projects/monolith/frontend/src/lib/public/ember/ember.css`, which is one tier
darker than its reference mockup for exactly this reason.

### Aesthetic Direction

This repo converges on **one design system, the technical drawing**. Joe
decided it on 2026-10-17 after the exploration in
[#6822](https://github.com/jomcgi-org/homelab/issues/6822); the rollout is
tracked surface by surface in
[#7016](https://github.com/jomcgi-org/homelab/issues/7016). The language
began as the private-tier document style, then carried the public blog and
the slop factory, and on 2026-10-30 every remaining surface moved to it:
the public apps, the Ember pages and stories, the private tools and the
friends screens render on the sheet tokens, in both schemes, and the
brutalist site nav is gone. Until 2026-10-17 the repo ran four separate
systems on purpose; the three others are legacy. Their rules stay recorded
below, with every veto, so a maintenance change inside what is left of one
stays coherent, and nothing new starts on them. What is left: the brutalist
stylesheet on the root error page (its removal waits on the Grimoire
rehearsal), the Ember stylesheet as the figure-tone source of the two
stories, and the Grimoire ledger values for a `.grimoire` tree outside a
sheet, of which none remain (#7160).

The shared `@homelab/design-system` package (approved 2026-10-06 in #6822
and [#6873](https://github.com/jomcgi-org/homelab/issues/6873)) carries
opt-in controls and data-display primitives over `--ds-*` roles. Inside a
sheet those roles are derived from the page tokens (the bridge in
`technical-drawing.css`), so a primitive dropped on any `.td` surface
follows the sheet and its scheme toggle without a `data-ds-theme`
boundary; the package's own themes remain the boundaries for a surface
outside a sheet.

#### 1. The shared language: technical drawing

Source of truth:
`projects/monolith/frontend/src/lib/public/styles/technical-drawing.css`.
It holds the tokens, the `--ds-*` bridge and the `.sheet` page vocabulary
that every migrated page is built from. The shared chrome lives in
`src/lib/public/components/`: `Trail` (where the reader is), `SiteIndex`
(the site's sections, in the spine), `TechnicalDrawingChrome` and
`SchemeToggle` (the day/night switch, persisted as `data-theme` on the
root). The blog, the slop factory and the private updates journal were the
reference implementations and still carry private copies of the vocabulary;
they adopt `.sheet` and drop those copies last (#7016).

A page's anatomy, in the class names the sheet defines:

- `main.td.sheet` grounds the page, then `.frame` caps the width at spine
  plus gap plus a 54em measure, and `.journal` lays the two out. A page
  with nothing to put in a spine uses `.journal.single`.
- `.spine` is sticky and holds, in order: `.trail-dock` (the trail), the
  page's own `.index` (a `/ Index` label over `.index-list` entries,
  `.index-group` details for long lists, `.strip` to become the sticky
  strip on a phone), then `SiteIndex`. On a phone the trail moves into the
  chrome row, the strip index pins to the top and the rest of the spine
  hides.
- Content is `.edition` panels, spaced in `.editions` or collapsed into one
  ruled list with `.editions.ruled`. A panel is `.ed-head` (the band:
  mono, sentence case, `.num` for a record number, `.end` for what parks
  right, `.tabs` for a divided tab strip), `.ed-lead` (headline and lead),
  `.ed-body` (optionally `.prose` for rendered markdown), `.spec` rows
  (mono label column, value flowing left), `.divided` rows (one divided
  value) and `.ed-foot` (a row of links).
- Diagrams are keyed figures: `Diagram`, `DBox`, `DGroup` and `DArrow`
  under `components/diagrams/` draw parts as outlines partitioned by
  rules, with a caption row the figure counter numbers.

The rules below are the language itself, recorded as they were decided on
the private tier.

#### The homepage: one Braun unit

Joe chose this on 2026-10-20 after a run of artifact studies, over the
sheet's panel-and-spine layout, which read as a docs index. `/` is one
object, read top to bottom like a Braun calculator: a nameplate
(domain, Scotland clock, deploy age, the on light with the live sha), a
dark display window, a grille with the portrait behind it, a keypad of
nine round keys with lowercase mono legends, and the two latest posts as
legends under the keypad. The sheet's own tokens supply every tone
(`--band` is the housing, `--sheet` the key caps and the plate behind the
grille, `--ok` the one coloured key); only the display's dark glass has
values of its own. The unit is the one public surface with radii and hard
offset shadows, because it is drawn as a product, and that language stays
inside `src/routes/public/+page.svelte`.

- **The display is the only thing that changes.** It is a fixed box.
  Whatever the visitor hovers (a key, a post) is read out there, name on
  the top line and one sentence below, and it returns to the name on
  leave. Hover is mouse-only; touch uses the press. Nothing else on the
  unit moves on hover, so the layout never shifts. In-key reveals, a
  reader line under the grid and tall label-plus-text keys were all tried
  and vetoed as noisy or janky.
- **The portrait is uneasy; the chrome is still.** The cut-out
  (`static/joe-grey.png`, background removed, greyscale) is printed in
  the scheme's ramp on a canvas and cut into 56 strips with spring
  physics (`home-portrait.js`): a fast pointer shoves it aside and it
  zips back, it drifts when ignored, comes apart when the pointer leaves
  the window, and a glitch clock cuts into it on exponential gaps that
  quicken from calm to restless (3 s idle) to bored (30 s), when it also
  re-inks in the figure tones. Keys that leaned, scattered or rotated
  were tried and vetoed: the discomfort lives in the picture, the
  controls stay flat and clickable. Reduced motion gets the still print.
- **On a phone the unit is one screen.** Joe chose this on 2026-10-21:
  a hardware object should not scroll. Under 760 px the unit fills the
  visible viewport (`dvh`), the portrait and keypad share the deck at the
  height the nameplate, glass and footer leave, and the writing legends
  drop (the footer's blog link leads to them). Spare height is grille,
  never empty housing: side by side, the grille frame runs the deck's
  height and the picture fills it, cropped at the sides like a tight
  portrait (headroom above a small picture was tried and looked broken);
  on a deck taller than 4:3 the grille spans the width above a full-width
  keypad. A screen too
  short for the keypad, such as a phone on its side, scrolls rather than
  crushing the keys.
- **Copy is Joe's.** The name line and the sub line are his words; each
  key's sentence is a draft in the register of the posts (blunt fact,
  plain mechanism, no adjectives about the work).

#### Legacy 2. Public tier: neobrutalist (`jomcgi.dev`)

**Legacy since 2026-10-17; no page renders it since 2026-10-30.** The CV,
the apps under `/app/*` and the shared `Nav` were its last consumers; the
nav retired and the rest moved to the sheet. `design-system.css` stays
imported by the root error page for the reason below and leaves the tree
with #7160. The rules stay recorded for that retirement and for reading
the older artefacts.

**The sheet also reaches the friends and private tiers by accident, and
they depend on it.** `src/routes/+error.svelte` imports it, SvelteKit's
client preloads the root error node on every page, and so the Grimoire
session screen and the private tools get its reset, body font and `.btn`
vocabulary without importing it. Removing that import (tried in #7017) set
the Grimoire session screen in the browser's serif and broke its phone
layout. The import stays on the error page until those tiers carry their
own language; retiring `design-system.css` is the last step of #7016, not
a side effect of the public pages moving.

Source of truth: `projects/monolith/frontend/src/lib/public/styles/design-system.css`
Self-described as "MotherDuck-inspired brutalist".

- **Surface:** cream paper `--cream: #f3ede1`, white panels `--paper`, ink
  `--ink: #1a1a1a`.
- **Accents:** yellow `--accent: #ffde01`, blue `--blue: #6fc2ff`, coral
  `--coral: #ff7169`, teal, green.
- **Borders: two levels, and the distinction matters.** `2px solid var(--ink)`
  for component outlines (buttons, cards, panels), and `1px solid var(--rule)`
  or `var(--rule-2)` for internal dividers and table rows. Do not draw row
  separators in 2px ink: roughly 20 one-pixel `--rule` borders already exist
  across the public tier (llm-leaderboard tables, HikesMap, StarsMap, DocsShell).
- **Shadows:** hard offsets only (`--shadow-hard: 4px 4px 0 var(--ink)`).
  **Blur is forbidden here.** A hard offset implies a single hard light on a flat
  plane; a blurred shadow implies ambient depth and instantly reads as generic
  Material. This is the single most load-bearing rule in the system. It is
  descriptive, not aspirational: two component comments independently restate it
  (`DayMap.svelte`, the trips day page). One legacy exception survives, the 10px
  photo-scrub marker dot in `DayElevationProfile.svelte`, which also transitions
  `left`/`top` against principle 4. Treat it as debt to fix, never as precedent.
- **Type:** Instrument Serif for display (`letter-spacing: -0.02em`,
  `line-height: 0.95`), Hanken Grotesk for body, JetBrains Mono for code and
  eyebrows (uppercase, `0.14em` tracking).
- **Motion is physical.** Buttons translate `-3px` and grow a hard shadow on
  hover, then snap flat on `:active`, simulating an object lifting off paper and
  being pressed back down. Cards lift `-2px`. `.hl` highlights sweep in with
  `scaleX` from a left origin. `.reveal` staggers at 80/160/240ms.
- **Class vocabulary:** `.btn` / `.btn-primary` / `.btn-secondary`, `.card-hard`,
  `.display`, `.eyebrow`, `.hl` / `.hl-yellow` / `.hl-blue` / `.hl-coral`,
  `.mono`, `.page`, `.reveal`, `.wrap` / `.wrap-narrow`.

#### Legacy 3. Ember mini-site: warm technical (`/ember/*`)

**Legacy since 2026-10-17; migrated on 2026-10-30.** The landing, the
Bazel and Postgres fallbacks and the Firecracker and agents stories render
on the sheet. `ember.css` remains only as the source of the stories' figure
tones and goes with #7160; the semantic colour families below are what
carried across.

Source of truth: `projects/monolith/frontend/src/lib/public/ember/ember.css`,
scoped under `.ember-site`.

- **Deliberately not brutalist.** Soft layered shadows
  (`0 2px 6px` plus `0 18px 44px`), warm ground `--em-ground: #f7f5f0`, system
  font stacks rather than webfonts. It reads as its own small site sitting next
  to the neobrutalist baseline.
- **`src/lib/public/fcstory/fcstory.css` is the sibling stylesheet**, styling the
  `/ember/firecracker` scroll story. `ember.css` defines itself relative to it
  ("same palette, type stack, and shadow language ... so the demo pages and the
  explainer read as one small site"), and the two divergences below are stated as
  deltas from it. Restyling `/ember/firecracker` means editing that file.
- **Semantic color families are load-bearing, not decorative.** Ember red
  `--em-ember: #e0421a` is reserved for the "bazel says" error box. Frost blue
  `--em-frost` is cold or banked state. Green `--em-good` exists specifically so
  a positive metric never borrows the error red. Amber is intermediate.
  Respect these assignments: recoloring across families breaks the explanation
  the page is making.
- Overscroll is pinned via `html:has(.ember-site)` so the brutalist background
  never shows through the rubber-band area.

#### Legacy 4. Grimoire: arcane ledger (`/app/grimoire`)

**Legacy since 2026-10-17; bridged on 2026-10-30.** The public reader
moved to the sheet (`src/lib/public/grimoire/sheet.css`), and the friends
campaign screens and the private character sheets sit on it through a
bridge at the end of `theme.css`: inside a `.td` scope every `--grim-*`
role resolves to a sheet token (paper and surfaces to the sheet, lines to
the stroke, the slate accent to the accent ink, the serif to the grotesk,
the dark nav to the band), so one scheme toggle moves them with the page.
The entity-type palette stays as meaningful colour. The ledger values below
remain for a `.grimoire` tree outside a sheet; none remain, and they go
with #7160.

Source of truth: `projects/monolith/frontend/src/lib/grimoire/theme.css`, scoped
under `.grimoire`. Layers over the shared monolith tokens and never forks them.

- The stated intent is "an arcane ledger, not a terminal": keep mono for chrome
  (labels, nav, badges), add a serif display face for names and titles, tint the
  reading pane like paper.
- **Slate-blue accent** `--grim-accent: #33507a` over light neutral paper
  `--grim-paper: #f3f5f7`. This replaced an earlier oxblood-and-parchment scheme
  in the 2026-07-05 reskin; do not reintroduce oxblood. **`theme.css`'s own
  comments are stale on this point**: the file header still says "swap the blue
  accent for oxblood" and the focus-ring comment still says "the oxblood accent",
  both describing the pre-reskin palette. The code is right and those two
  comments are wrong. Do not "correct" this document to match them.
- **Serif is a system stack** (Iowan Old Style, Palatino, Georgia) on purpose: it
  ships no binary, has no font-loading gotcha, and every listed family supports
  the small-caps and old-style figures the stat blocks want. Swapping to a
  licensed self-hosted serif is a drop-in change to `--grim-serif`.
- **The nav is dark** (`--grim-nav-surface: #1c1712`) deliberately breaking from
  the light reading surface, so the topbar reads as the book's spine rather than
  a generic light SaaS header.
- Entity-type colors are a stable semantic palette; mechanics types alias to one
  shared slate hue rather than repeating values.

#### The language's rules, as decided on the private tier

Reference implementation:
`projects/monolith/frontend/src/routes/private/updates/+page.svelte` (chosen
2026-08-30 after two rejected drafts: a conservative polish pass and a warm
serif editorial layout, both vetoed for wasted negative space and unclear
narrative). The public blog at `jomcgi.dev/blog` (`src/routes/public/blog/`)
and the slop factory followed it, and the shared sheet above lifts their
vocabulary. The public token file is a deliberate copy of the private one
because the public build excludes `lib/private/`; change
`src/lib/public/styles/technical-drawing.css` and
`lib/private/dashboard-theme.css` together.

The keyed exploded-view figure is the tier's diagram form. Figures are inline
SVG under `docs/posts/figures/`, following the conventions in
`docs/posts/README.md`: numbered callouts, mono labels, `currentColor` strokes,
one fixed tone per memory tier (GPU, host RAM, page cache, NVMe, hot set)
shared by every figure, and a key table docked under the caption.
An operating sequence holds lettered parts in place across numbered panels and
uses solid arrows for movement and dashed lines for advisory or asynchronous paths.

Reference shelf, each with what it contributes here:

- **Rams, "Weniger, aber besser."** The tier's governing rule (see Brand
  Personality) applied literally: as little design as possible, every visual
  element is structure, ornament is a budget not a default.
- **Tufte, "The Visual Display of Quantitative Information."** Data-ink
  ratio: a rule, box, or label exists only to carry information, and the
  information density can be high when the chrome is quiet. The boxed
  metadata values, the count columns, and the rules-end-where-text-ends
  principle are all this.
- **Haynes, "Motorcycle Basics Techbook."** The register for the writing and
  any future figures: dense, plain 1990s technical prose, numbered
  procedures, captioned exploded-view diagrams with keyed part labels. When
  a docs page needs a diagram, it should look like a keyed figure with mono
  captions, not an illustration.
- **Web brutalism and Stripe's dev-site document pages.** The concrete
  markup vocabulary: mono slash-labels, boxed values, visible grid,
  keyboard-plain navigation.

- **Tokens come from `lib/private/dashboard-theme.css`**, but document pages
  use a clean light/dark pair only: `shell day` for light and `shell night`
  for dark, mapped from `prefers-color-scheme` (SSR renders light, a
  matchMedia listener corrects on hydrate). The dawn/dusk drift and the glow
  gradients were tried here and vetoed: ambient tinting suits the dashboard,
  not a technical record. One tier token and three page-local derived tokens
  are the only new color surface allowed: `--sheet` in dashboard-theme.css
  (true paper white by day, neutral dark by night, because `--card-bg`
  reads as cream next to white), and per page
  `--accent-ink: color-mix(in srgb, var(--accent) 85%, var(--ink))` so
  small accent chrome sits well above AA on both schemes,
  `--stroke: color-mix(in srgb, var(--ink) 28%, transparent)` because `--line`
  is too faint to carry structure on the night palette, and
  `--band: color-mix(in srgb, var(--ink) 5%, var(--sheet))` for header
  strips so bands stay in the sheet's neutral family.
- **Rule hierarchy carries the drawing.** 1px ink for the panel outline,
  `--stroke` for internal partitions, `--line` for hairline row separators
  and cell dividers. No shadows, no radii, no gradients, no fills except
  the accent markers and the `--band` strips.
- **One heading style for all chrome:** the `/ Section` label, mono
  (`--font-code`) uppercase over a 1px `--stroke` rule, used only in the
  spine (`/ Filter`, `/ Index`, `/ Status`); edition chrome inside panels
  is sentence-case mono bands instead. Content headlines are `--font-ui`
  (Schibsted Grotesk) at weight 700, tight tracking, document scale
  (max ~1.35rem), never display-serif.
- **Metadata rows are a spec table:** a fixed mono label column (Filed,
  Source) with its own vertical rule, the value flowing LEFT beside it
  (right-aligned values wrap ragged against a gulf and were vetoed), one
  hairline per row. The edition head band carries the date alone: the
  category label was near-constant and removed as noise. Other vetoed
  shapes: a side metadata column (stranded empty space beside long
  articles), boxed chip rows (chip soup), a wrapping key-value band (buried
  the headline), and a slash-labeled edition banner (a second masthead's
  worth of noise saying nothing the date alone did not).
- **Selection marker language:** a 2px `--accent-ink` left bar on vertical
  lists, becoming a bottom underline in horizontal strips (a left bar in a
  horizontal row reads as a divider). Active text also turns `--accent-ink`.
- **Long option lists collapse:** native `<details>/<summary>` groups,
  closed by default, `open` when a selection inside them is active, `+`/`−`
  indicator on the count. Navigation (the index) must stay above the fold.
- **AA constraints:** `--ink-3` is for ornament labels only, never interactive
  or meaningful text (2.78:1 on day paper); interactive text floor is
  `--ink-2`; verify every choice against both schemes, night is the trap.
- **Content lives in outlined panels.** Each record (an edition) is one
  1px ink-bordered panel with internal 1px `--stroke` partitions between
  its header strip, lead, metadata rows, and sections. Text floating in
  open space between horizontal rules was tried and vetoed twice: rules
  alone read as noise, panels read as a diagram.
- **No in-flow masthead at all.** The tier's fixed overlay back link is
  the only chrome (it costs no page space) and the page h1 is
  screen-reader-only, so content starts at the top of the viewport. Both a
  title crumb with a full-width rule and an in-flow back-link band were
  tried and vetoed as noise that helped nothing.
- **Repeated values render as one divided value.** Tag lists live inside
  a full-width table row, separated by 1px vertical divider lines; the row
  supplies the frame. Individual gapped chips, collapsed-border cell
  grids, and dot-separated prose were each tried and vetoed.
- **The page is one bright sheet.** The whole page grounds on `--sheet`
  (panels floating on cream paper and grounding on the warm `--card-bg`
  were both tried and rejected: the sheet is a whole, and card warmth
  reads as cream); header strips (the date row, section labels) get the
  `--band` tint, so structure reads from ground color before the rules
  are even parsed. The page also pins the root font to 16px via the same
  `html:has` rule that pins the overscroll ground: the tier scales the
  html root with the viewport, and a fixed document needs a fixed basis.
- **Rules end where text ends.** The sheet width is spine + gap + a ~48em
  text measure, so no rule stroke runs past the content it delimits; a rule
  implying content that is not there was the wasted-space complaint that
  forced this cap.
- **Ornament budget: zero.** Registration crosses on the sheet corners
  were tried and removed: ornament never wins a collision with function.
  Form follows function, Braun-style: if an element is not a control, a
  label, or content, it does not render.

The rest of the private tier (the dashboard's warm serif landing page, the
older mono tools) predates this language and migrates under #7016.

**Former trap, fixed as a cascade problem rather than a tier rewrite.** The two
token sets use five of the same names with different values: `--accent`,
`--bg`, `--coral`, `--cream`, and `--green`. `src/routes/+layout.svelte` still
imports `global.css` on every route, and Grimoire's chrome still receives shared
tokens such as `--font-mono` through it. The public values for the five
conflicting names now live on `body:has(.public-theme)`, keyed by a layout-owned
marker on public routes and the same marker on the root error boundary. That
scope has higher specificity than the shared `:root` defaults, so stylesheet
import order cannot flip the brutalist palette, and client navigation away from
the public layout removes its marker. Grimoire and Ember keep their nested
`.grimoire` and `.ember-site` theme scopes.

### Design Principles

**1. Function is the aesthetic.**
"Less but better." Every visual element justifies itself by what it communicates.
If a shadow, animation, border, or color is not carrying information or
affordance, remove it. Looking nice is permitted and welcome; it is never the
reason something stays.

**2. One language, scoped; a legacy system stays scoped until it goes.**
New work is a sheet: `class="td sheet"`, the shared vocabulary, the shared
components. A legacy surface keeps its root class (`.ember-site`,
`.grimoire`, the public baseline) until its migration PR, and a maintenance
change inside one follows that system's recorded rules rather than mixing
two languages on one page. Pin the overscroll background via `:has()` so
the wrong background never bleeds through.

**3. No NEW bare hex; color belongs in the token file.**
Components should reference `var(--*)`, and adding a color means adding a named
token with a comment explaining what it is for. Nothing enforces it: the
repo's custom Semgrep rules, including `svelte-hardcoded-color-in-style`, were
removed (#4777). A bare hex reaches main unchallenged, and the convention holds
only because people follow it. The `nosemgrep` comments across the monolith
frontend, public-tier files included, are leftovers from that rule and suppress
nothing, so this is a rule about new code, not a claim about the current tree.

**4. Motion is physical, purposeful, and optional.**
Motion should model something real: an object lifting, a highlight being drawn, a
row arriving. Transform and opacity only. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion`
everywhere, and prefer Grimoire's stricter opt-in shape
(`@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`) for new work, so motion is
absent by default rather than suppressed after the fact.

**5. WCAG 2.2 AA is the floor, and contrast fixes get recorded.**
Body text clears 4.5:1, large text 3:1, every interaction is keyboard operable,
and focus is always visible (themed per system, never the UA default blue). When
a color is darkened to pass, leave the comment saying what it was and why it
moved, as `--grim-text-faint` does. Watch the known-risky spots: cream-on-cream
fills, low-opacity highlight underlays, and any small label on a tinted surface.

**6. Write the rationale into the CSS, including what was rejected.**
This codebase's stylesheets already explain why `--btn-secondary` is white fill
rather than transparent, why blur is banned, and why `--em-muted` is one tier
darker than its mockup. That habit is why the systems have stayed coherent.
Preserve it: a comment recording a vetoed alternative prevents the next person
from reintroducing it.
