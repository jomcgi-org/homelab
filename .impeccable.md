# Impeccable

Design guidance for this repo. Gathered by `/impeccable:teach-impeccable`.

## Design Context

### Users

The public tier (`jomcgi.dev`) is built for a **technical audience evaluating the
work on its merits**: peers, collaborators, and anyone professionally assessing
engineering depth.

That audience shapes every decision. They arrive skimming, they leave in under a
minute if nothing catches, and they judge competence from the artifact itself
rather than from a claim about it. The job to be done is: *make the depth legible
fast, without requiring the visitor to read code.* A working demo beats a
description of a demo. A measured number (`~25 ms request to warm semgrep scan`)
beats an adjective. Craft matters because sloppiness reads as a signal about the
engineering behind it.

Two reading modes to serve, in priority order:

1. **The skimmer**, who has a minute and wants to know whether there is substance
   here. Optimize for this one. Headline, working demo, and a real number carry
   the weight.
2. **The depth reader**, who will actually open an ADR or an STPA model. Reward
   them with density and precision once they commit, but never at the cost of the
   skimmer's first minute.

A third group arrives for one specific tool (the ships map, the hike finder).
For them the design should get out of the way entirely and let the tool work.

### Brand Personality

**Playful, rigorous, fast.** Three words that pull against each other on purpose,
held together by one governing rule:

> Less but better. It can look nice, but ultimately function is beautiful.

That is the tiebreaker whenever the three pull apart. Decoration that does not
carry information loses. An animation that does not communicate state loses. The
yellow-and-coral energy of the public tier is welcome precisely because it costs
nothing: it is flat fills and hard borders, not gradient meshes and blur.

- **Playful, characterful.** Willing to be memorable and slightly irreverent.
  Brutalist yellow, a marquee ticker, a highlight that sweeps in. Never at the
  expense of clarity.
- **Rigorous, no fluff.** Confidence through evident care. Claims are backed by
  real numbers from real hardware. Nothing decorative that is not earning its
  place.
- **Fast and utilitarian.** Speed is part of the aesthetic. Chrome is minimal.
  The tools work instantly or they have failed.

Anti-pattern to avoid: **generic SaaS**. Soft blurred shadows, a purple gradient
hero, rounded pill everything, a stock illustration of people collaborating. The
whole visual language is a deliberate rejection of that, and drifting back toward
it is the main risk in any redesign.

Second anti-pattern, recorded from real feedback: **washed-out muted greys**.
Grey-on-beige secondary text has been vetoed twice on this codebase (once on the
homepage, once on the ember mini-site). Secondary text stays secondary through
size and weight, never through washout. See `--em-muted` in
`projects/monolith/frontend/src/lib/public/ember/ember.css`, which is one tier
darker than its reference mockup for exactly this reason.

### Aesthetic Direction

This repo runs **three separate design systems**, deliberately distinct. They are
not stages of a migration and should not be converged. Each is scoped in CSS so
they can coexist on one origin.

#### 1. Public tier: neobrutalist (`jomcgi.dev`)

Source of truth: `projects/monolith/frontend/src/lib/public/styles/design-system.css`
Self-described as "MotherDuck-inspired brutalist".

- **Surface:** cream paper `--cream: #f3ede1`, white panels `--paper`, ink
  `--ink: #1a1a1a`.
- **Accents:** yellow `--accent: #ffde01`, blue `--blue: #6fc2ff`, coral
  `--coral: #ff7169`, teal, green.
- **Borders:** flat `2px solid var(--ink)`. No hairlines.
- **Shadows:** hard offsets only (`--shadow-hard: 4px 4px 0 var(--ink)`).
  **Blur is forbidden here.** A hard offset implies a single hard light on a flat
  plane; a blurred shadow implies ambient depth and instantly reads as generic
  Material. This is the single most load-bearing rule in the system.
- **Type:** Instrument Serif for display (`letter-spacing: -0.02em`,
  `line-height: 0.95`), Hanken Grotesk for body, JetBrains Mono for code and
  eyebrows (uppercase, `0.14em` tracking).
- **Motion is physical.** Buttons translate `-3px` and grow a hard shadow on
  hover, then snap flat on `:active`, simulating an object lifting off paper and
  being pressed back down. Cards lift `-2px`. `.hl` highlights sweep in with
  `scaleX` from a left origin. `.reveal` staggers at 80/160/240ms.
- **Class vocabulary:** `.btn` / `.btn-primary` / `.btn-secondary`, `.card-hard`,
  `.display`, `.eyebrow`, `.hl` / `.hl-yellow` / `.hl-blue` / `.hl-coral`,
  `.mono`, `.page`, `.reveal`, `.wrap` / `.wrap-narrow`.

#### 2. Ember mini-site: warm technical (`/ember/*`)

Source of truth: `projects/monolith/frontend/src/lib/public/ember/ember.css`,
scoped under `.ember-site`. Paired with `ember-stage.css` for the RAM cell grid.

- **Deliberately not brutalist.** Soft layered shadows
  (`0 2px 6px` plus `0 18px 44px`), warm ground `--em-ground: #f7f5f0`, system
  font stacks rather than webfonts. It reads as its own small site sitting next
  to the neobrutalist baseline.
- **Semantic color families are load-bearing, not decorative.** Ember red
  `--em-ember: #e0421a` is reserved for the "bazel says" error box. Frost blue
  `--em-frost` is cold or banked state. Green `--em-good` exists specifically so
  a positive metric never borrows the error red. Amber is intermediate.
  Respect these assignments: recoloring across families breaks the explanation
  the page is making.
- Overscroll is pinned via `html:has(.ember-site)` so the brutalist background
  never shows through the rubber-band area.

#### 3. Grimoire: arcane ledger (`/app/grimoire`)

Source of truth: `projects/monolith/frontend/src/lib/grimoire/theme.css`, scoped
under `.grimoire`. Layers over the shared monolith tokens and never forks them.

- The stated intent is "an arcane ledger, not a terminal": keep mono for chrome
  (labels, nav, badges), add a serif display face for names and titles, tint the
  reading pane like paper.
- **Slate-blue accent** `--grim-accent: #33507a` over light neutral paper
  `--grim-paper: #f3f5f7`. This replaced an earlier oxblood-and-parchment scheme
  in the 2026-07-05 reskin; do not reintroduce oxblood.
- **Serif is a system stack** (Iowan Old Style, Palatino, Georgia) on purpose: it
  ships no binary, has no font-loading gotcha, and every listed family supports
  the small-caps and old-style figures the stat blocks want. Swapping to a
  licensed self-hosted serif is a drop-in change to `--grim-serif`.
- **The nav is dark** (`--grim-nav-surface: #1c1712`) deliberately breaking from
  the light reading surface, so the topbar reads as the book's spine rather than
  a generic light SaaS header.
- Entity-type colors are a stable semantic palette; mechanics types alias to one
  shared slate hue rather than repeating values.

#### Note on the other two surfaces

The **private tier** (`private.jomcgi.dev`, tokens at
`src/lib/styles/shared/tokens.css`) is a stark monochrome mono system with a real
`body.dark` theme. It is a personal tool and is out of scope for this design
context.

`projects/grimoire/frontend/` is a **separate React prototype** with its own
inline JS token constants (`src/lib/tokens.ts`). It is not the Grimoire described
above, and it is not covered here.

**Trap:** the public and private tiers use the same token *names* with different
values, and in one case with a different hue entirely. `--accent` is yellow
`#ffde01` on public and blue `#0066ff` on private. `--coral` is `#ff7169` versus
`#ff6b5b`, `--cream` is `#f3ede1` versus `#f1ebdc`, `--green` is `#4ade80` versus
`#5dd879`. Never assume a token name means the same color across tiers.

### Design Principles

**1. Function is the aesthetic.**
"Less but better." Every visual element justifies itself by what it communicates.
If a shadow, animation, border, or color is not carrying information or
affordance, remove it. Looking nice is permitted and welcome; it is never the
reason something stays.

**2. Each system stays separate, scoped, and layered.**
Three design systems coexist because each serves a different argument. Scope new
work under the right root class (`.ember-site`, `.grimoire`, or the public
baseline), layer over shared tokens rather than forking them, and pin the
overscroll background via `:has()` so the wrong background never bleeds through.
Do not unify them.

**3. All literal color lives in the token file.**
Components reference `var(--*)` exclusively; no bare hex in a `.svelte` `<style>`
block. This is enforced by the semgrep rule `svelte-hardcoded-color-in-style`,
not merely encouraged. Adding a color means adding a named token with a comment
explaining what it is for.

**4. Motion is physical, purposeful, and optional.**
Motion should model something real: an object lifting, a highlight being drawn, a
row arriving. Transform and opacity only. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion`
everywhere, and prefer Grimoire's stricter opt-in shape
(`@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`) for new work, so motion is
absent by default rather than suppressed after the fact.

**5. WCAG 2.2 AA is the floor, and contrast fixes get recorded.**
Body text clears 4.5:1, large text 3:1, every interaction is keyboard operable,
and focus is always visible (themed per system, never the UA default blue). When
a color is darkened to pass, leave the comment saying what it was and why it
moved, as `--grim-text-faint` does. Watch the known-risky spots: cream-on-cream
fills, low-opacity highlight underlays, and any small label on a tinted surface.

**6. Write the rationale into the CSS, including what was rejected.**
This codebase's stylesheets already explain why `--btn-secondary` is white fill
rather than transparent, why blur is banned, and why `--em-muted` is one tier
darker than its mockup. That habit is why the systems have stayed coherent.
Preserve it: a comment recording a vetoed alternative prevents the next person
from reintroducing it.
