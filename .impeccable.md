# Impeccable

Design guidance for this repo. Gathered by `/impeccable:teach-impeccable`.

## Design Context

### Users

The public tier (`jomcgi.dev`) is built for a **technical audience evaluating the
work on its merits**: peers, collaborators, and anyone professionally assessing
engineering depth.

That audience shapes every decision. They arrive skimming, they leave in under a
minute if nothing catches, and they judge competence from the artifact itself
rather than from a claim about it. The job to be done is: *make the depth legible
fast, without requiring the visitor to read code.* A working demo beats a
description of a demo. A measured number (`~25 ms request to warm semgrep scan`)
beats an adjective. Craft matters because sloppiness reads as a signal about the
engineering behind it.

Two reading modes to serve, in priority order:

1. **The skimmer**, who has a minute and wants to know whether there is substance
   here. Optimize for this one. Headline, working demo, and a real number carry
   the weight.
2. **The depth reader**, who will actually open an ADR or an STPA model. Reward
   them with density and precision once they commit, but never at the cost of the
   skimmer's first minute.

A third group arrives for one specific tool (the ships map, the hike finder).
For them the design should get out of the way entirely and let the tool work.

### Brand Personality

**Playful, rigorous, fast.** Three words that pull against each other on purpose,
held together by one governing rule:

> Less but better. It can look nice, but ultimately function is beautiful.

That is the tiebreaker whenever the three pull apart. Decoration that does not
carry information loses. An animation that does not communicate state loses. The
yellow-and-coral energy of the public tier is welcome precisely because it costs
nothing: it is flat fills and hard borders, not gradient meshes and blur.

- **Playful, characterful.** Willing to be memorable and slightly irreverent.
  Brutalist yellow, a marquee ticker, a highlight that sweeps in. Never at the
  expense of clarity.
- **Rigorous, no fluff.** Confidence through evident care. Claims are backed by
  real numbers from real hardware. Nothing decorative that is not earning its
  place.
- **Fast and utilitarian.** Speed is part of the aesthetic. Chrome is minimal.
  The tools work instantly or they have failed.

Anti-pattern to avoid: **generic SaaS**. Soft blurred shadows, a purple gradient
hero, rounded pill everything, a stock illustration of people collaborating. The
whole visual language is a deliberate rejection of that, and drifting back toward
it is the main risk in any redesign.

Second anti-pattern, recorded from real feedback: **washed-out muted greys**.
Grey-on-beige secondary text has been vetoed twice on this codebase (once on the
homepage, once on the ember mini-site). Secondary text stays secondary through
size and weight, never through washout. See `--em-muted` in
`projects/monolith/frontend/src/lib/public/ember/ember.css`, which is one tier
darker than its reference mockup for exactly this reason.

### Aesthetic Direction

This repo runs **four separate design systems**, deliberately distinct. They are
not stages of a migration and should not be converged. Each is scoped in CSS so
they can coexist on one origin.

#### 1. Public tier: neobrutalist (`jomcgi.dev`)

Source of truth: `projects/monolith/frontend/src/lib/public/styles/design-system.css`
Self-described as "MotherDuck-inspired brutalist".

- **Surface:** cream paper `--cream: #f3ede1`, white panels `--paper`, ink
  `--ink: #1a1a1a`.
- **Accents:** yellow `--accent: #ffde01`, blue `--blue: #6fc2ff`, coral
  `--coral: #ff7169`, teal, green.
- **Borders: two levels, and the distinction matters.** `2px solid var(--ink)`
  for component outlines (buttons, cards, panels), and `1px solid var(--rule)`
  or `var(--rule-2)` for internal dividers and table rows. Do not draw row
  separators in 2px ink: roughly 20 one-pixel `--rule` borders already exist
  across the public tier (llm-leaderboard tables, HikesMap, StarsMap, DocsShell).
- **Shadows:** hard offsets only (`--shadow-hard: 4px 4px 0 var(--ink)`).
  **Blur is forbidden here.** A hard offset implies a single hard light on a flat
  plane; a blurred shadow implies ambient depth and instantly reads as generic
  Material. This is the single most load-bearing rule in the system. It is
  descriptive, not aspirational: two component comments independently restate it
  (`DayMap.svelte`, the trips day page). One legacy exception survives, the 10px
  photo-scrub marker dot in `DayElevationProfile.svelte`, which also transitions
  `left`/`top` against principle 4. Treat it as debt to fix, never as precedent.
- **Type:** Instrument Serif for display (`letter-spacing: -0.02em`,
  `line-height: 0.95`), Hanken Grotesk for body, JetBrains Mono for code and
  eyebrows (uppercase, `0.14em` tracking).
- **Motion is physical.** Buttons translate `-3px` and grow a hard shadow on
  hover, then snap flat on `:active`, simulating an object lifting off paper and
  being pressed back down. Cards lift `-2px`. `.hl` highlights sweep in with
  `scaleX` from a left origin. `.reveal` staggers at 80/160/240ms.
- **Class vocabulary:** `.btn` / `.btn-primary` / `.btn-secondary`, `.card-hard`,
  `.display`, `.eyebrow`, `.hl` / `.hl-yellow` / `.hl-blue` / `.hl-coral`,
  `.mono`, `.page`, `.reveal`, `.wrap` / `.wrap-narrow`.

#### 2. Ember mini-site: warm technical (`/ember/*`)

Source of truth: `projects/monolith/frontend/src/lib/public/ember/ember.css`,
scoped under `.ember-site`. Paired with `ember-stage.css` for the RAM cell grid.

- **Deliberately not brutalist.** Soft layered shadows
  (`0 2px 6px` plus `0 18px 44px`), warm ground `--em-ground: #f7f5f0`, system
  font stacks rather than webfonts. It reads as its own small site sitting next
  to the neobrutalist baseline.
- **`src/lib/public/fcstory/fcstory.css` is the sibling stylesheet**, styling the
  `/ember/firecracker` scroll story. `ember.css` defines itself relative to it
  ("same palette, type stack, and shadow language ... so the demo pages and the
  explainer read as one small site"), and the two divergences below are stated as
  deltas from it. Restyling `/ember/firecracker` means editing that file.
- **Semantic color families are load-bearing, not decorative.** Ember red
  `--em-ember: #e0421a` is reserved for the "bazel says" error box. Frost blue
  `--em-frost` is cold or banked state. Green `--em-good` exists specifically so
  a positive metric never borrows the error red. Amber is intermediate.
  Respect these assignments: recoloring across families breaks the explanation
  the page is making.
- Overscroll is pinned via `html:has(.ember-site)` so the brutalist background
  never shows through the rubber-band area.

#### 3. Grimoire: arcane ledger (`/app/grimoire`)

Source of truth: `projects/monolith/frontend/src/lib/grimoire/theme.css`, scoped
under `.grimoire`. Layers over the shared monolith tokens and never forks them.

- The stated intent is "an arcane ledger, not a terminal": keep mono for chrome
  (labels, nav, badges), add a serif display face for names and titles, tint the
  reading pane like paper.
- **Slate-blue accent** `--grim-accent: #33507a` over light neutral paper
  `--grim-paper: #f3f5f7`. This replaced an earlier oxblood-and-parchment scheme
  in the 2026-07-05 reskin; do not reintroduce oxblood. **`theme.css`'s own
  comments are stale on this point**: the file header still says "swap the blue
  accent for oxblood" and the focus-ring comment still says "the oxblood accent",
  both describing the pre-reskin palette. The code is right and those two
  comments are wrong. Do not "correct" this document to match them.
- **Serif is a system stack** (Iowan Old Style, Palatino, Georgia) on purpose: it
  ships no binary, has no font-loading gotcha, and every listed family supports
  the small-caps and old-style figures the stat blocks want. Swapping to a
  licensed self-hosted serif is a drop-in change to `--grim-serif`.
- **The nav is dark** (`--grim-nav-surface: #1c1712`) deliberately breaking from
  the light reading surface, so the topbar reads as the book's spine rather than
  a generic light SaaS header.
- Entity-type colors are a stable semantic palette; mechanics types alias to one
  shared slate hue rather than repeating values.

#### 4. Private tier documents: technical drawing (`private.jomcgi.dev`)

Reference implementation:
`projects/monolith/frontend/src/routes/private/updates/+page.svelte` (chosen
2026-08-30 after two rejected drafts: a conservative polish pass and a warm
serif editorial layout, both vetoed for wasted negative space and unclear
narrative). Use this language for any new private-tier page that presents
records or documentation, and for future docs surfaces that should read as
one system.

Reference shelf, each with what it contributes here:

- **Rams, "Weniger, aber besser."** The tier's governing rule (see Brand
  Personality) applied literally: as little design as possible, every visual
  element is structure, ornament is a budget not a default.
- **Tufte, "The Visual Display of Quantitative Information."** Data-ink
  ratio: a rule, box, or label exists only to carry information, and the
  information density can be high when the chrome is quiet. The boxed
  metadata values, the count columns, and the rules-end-where-text-ends
  principle are all this.
- **Haynes, "Motorcycle Basics Techbook."** The register for the writing and
  any future figures: dense, plain 1990s technical prose, numbered
  procedures, captioned exploded-view diagrams with keyed part labels. When
  a docs page needs a diagram, it should look like a keyed figure with mono
  captions, not an illustration.
- **Web brutalism and Stripe's dev-site document pages.** The concrete
  markup vocabulary: mono slash-labels, boxed values, visible grid,
  keyboard-plain navigation.

- **Tokens come from `lib/private/dashboard-theme.css`**, but document pages
  use a clean light/dark pair only: `shell day` for light and `shell night`
  for dark, mapped from `prefers-color-scheme` (SSR renders light, a
  matchMedia listener corrects on hydrate). The dawn/dusk drift and the glow
  gradients were tried here and vetoed: ambient tinting suits the dashboard,
  not a technical record. Two derived tokens are defined per page and are the
  only new color surface allowed:
  `--accent-ink: color-mix(in srgb, var(--accent) 85%, var(--ink))` because
  bare `--accent` fails WCAG AA at 3.85:1 on dawn paper, and
  `--stroke: color-mix(in srgb, var(--ink) 28%, transparent)` because `--line`
  is too faint to carry structure on the night palette.
- **Rule hierarchy carries the drawing.** `--stroke` for structural rules
  (section labels, the masthead, band boundaries), `--line` for hairline row
  separators. No shadows, no radii, no gradients, no fills except the accent
  markers.
- **One heading style for all chrome:** the `/ Section` label, mono
  (`--font-code`) 0.68rem uppercase over a 1px `--stroke` rule (`/ Filter`,
  `/ Index`, `/ Metadata`, `/ Edition · <date>`). Content headlines are
  `--font-ui` (Schibsted Grotesk) at weight 700, tight tracking, document
  scale (max ~2.2rem), never display-serif.
- **Metadata rows follow the spine's key-value discipline:** label left
  (sans, 600), value right-aligned mono, one hairline per row, the closing
  rule drawn by the next section label. The edition head is the same row:
  date left, category right in accent. Four earlier shapes were tried and
  vetoed: a side metadata column (stranded empty space beside long
  articles), boxed chip rows (chip soup), a wrapping key-value band (buried
  the headline), and a slash-labeled edition banner (a second masthead's
  worth of noise saying nothing the date alone did not).
- **Selection marker language:** a 2px `--accent-ink` left bar on vertical
  lists, becoming a bottom underline in horizontal strips (a left bar in a
  horizontal row reads as a divider). Active text also turns `--accent-ink`.
- **Long option lists collapse:** native `<details>/<summary>` groups,
  closed by default, `open` when a selection inside them is active, `+`/`−`
  indicator on the count. Navigation (the index) must stay above the fold.
- **AA constraints:** `--ink-3` is for ornament labels only, never interactive
  or meaningful text (2.78:1 on day paper); interactive text floor is
  `--ink-2`; verify every choice against all four period palettes, night and
  dawn are the traps.
- **Content lives in outlined panels.** Each record (an edition) is one
  1px ink-bordered panel with internal 1px `--stroke` partitions between
  its header strip, lead, metadata rows, and sections. Text floating in
  open space between horizontal rules was tried and vetoed twice: rules
  alone read as noise, panels read as a diagram.
- **No in-flow masthead at all.** The tier's fixed overlay back link is
  the only chrome (it costs no page space) and the page h1 is
  screen-reader-only, so content starts at the top of the viewport. Both a
  title crumb with a full-width rule and an in-flow back-link band were
  tried and vetoed as noise that helped nothing.
- **Repeated values render as one divided value.** Tag lists live inside
  a full-width table row, separated by 1px vertical divider lines; the row
  supplies the frame. Individual gapped chips, collapsed-border cell
  grids, and dot-separated prose were each tried and vetoed.
- **The page is one bright sheet.** The whole page grounds on
  `--card-bg` (panels floating on cream paper were tried and rejected:
  the sheet is a whole, not a section treatment); header strips (the
  date row, section labels) get a `--surface` tinted band, so structure
  reads from ground color before the rules are even parsed.
- **Rules end where text ends.** The sheet width is spine + gap + a ~48em
  text measure, so no rule stroke runs past the content it delimits; a rule
  implying content that is not there was the wasted-space complaint that
  forced this cap.
- **Ornament budget: zero.** Registration crosses on the sheet corners were
  tried and removed once the masthead topline became functional (back link
  left, folio right): they collided with it, and ornament never wins a
  collision with function. Form follows function, Braun-style: if an element
  is not a control, a label, or content, it does not render.

The rest of the private tier (the dashboard's warm serif landing page, the
older mono tools) predates this language; migrate surfaces to it when they are
next reworked rather than churning them.

**Trap, and it is a cascade problem rather than a tier problem.** The two token
sets use the same *names* with different values, and in one case a different hue
entirely: `--accent` is yellow `#ffde01` in `design-system.css` and blue
`#0066ff` in `tokens.css`. `--coral` is `#ff7169` versus `#ff6b5b`, `--cream` is
`#f3ede1` versus `#f1ebdc`, `--green` is `#4ade80` versus `#5dd879`.

They are not safely separated by tier. `src/routes/+layout.svelte` imports
`global.css` on **every** route, and `global.css`'s first line `@import`s
`styles/shared/tokens.css`. So public pages carry both token sets at once, and
`design-system.css` wins the overlapping names **only by CSS import order**.
Removing or reordering that import would silently flip `--accent` from yellow to
blue across the public tier. Grimoire's chrome also depends on the shared
`--font-mono` arriving this way on a public route. Before touching either
stylesheet's import position, work out which set is meant to win.

### Design Principles

**1. Function is the aesthetic.**
"Less but better." Every visual element justifies itself by what it communicates.
If a shadow, animation, border, or color is not carrying information or
affordance, remove it. Looking nice is permitted and welcome; it is never the
reason something stays.

**2. Each system stays separate, scoped, and layered.**
Three design systems coexist because each serves a different argument. Scope new
work under the right root class (`.ember-site`, `.grimoire`, or the public
baseline), layer over shared tokens rather than forking them, and pin the
overscroll background via `:has()` so the wrong background never bleeds through.
Do not unify them.

**3. No NEW bare hex; color belongs in the token file.**
Components should reference `var(--*)`, and adding a color means adding a named
token with a comment explaining what it is for. The semgrep rule
`svelte-hardcoded-color-in-style` is written to enforce this rather than merely
encourage it: `semgrep-test.sh` exits nonzero on any finding, regardless of the
rule's WARNING severity. Treat that as the intent, not as a gate you can rely
on today. Issue #4777 is open: every semgrep target currently exits 0 having
scanned nothing, so this rule and the other 92 enforce nothing at all. Until
that is fixed, a bare hex reaches main unchallenged and the convention holds
only because people follow it. The escape hatch also exists and is well used,
with 65
`nosemgrep` suppressions across the monolith frontend including public-tier
files, so this is a rule about new code, not a claim about the current tree.
Every suppression needs its `nosemgrep` comment. Two limits worth knowing: the
rule matches hex-after-colon anywhere in a `.svelte` file rather than only
inside `<style>` blocks, and it catches hex only, so `rgb()` and named colors
slip through.

**4. Motion is physical, purposeful, and optional.**
Motion should model something real: an object lifting, a highlight being drawn, a
row arriving. Transform and opacity only. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion`
everywhere, and prefer Grimoire's stricter opt-in shape
(`@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`) for new work, so motion is
absent by default rather than suppressed after the fact.

**5. WCAG 2.2 AA is the floor, and contrast fixes get recorded.**
Body text clears 4.5:1, large text 3:1, every interaction is keyboard operable,
and focus is always visible (themed per system, never the UA default blue). When
a color is darkened to pass, leave the comment saying what it was and why it
moved, as `--grim-text-faint` does. Watch the known-risky spots: cream-on-cream
fills, low-opacity highlight underlays, and any small label on a tinted surface.

**6. Write the rationale into the CSS, including what was rejected.**
This codebase's stylesheets already explain why `--btn-secondary` is white fill
rather than transparent, why blur is banned, and why `--em-muted` is one tier
darker than its mockup. That habit is why the systems have stayed coherent.
Preserve it: a comment recording a vetoed alternative prevents the next person
from reintroducing it.
