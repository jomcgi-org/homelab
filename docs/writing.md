# Writing for human readers

Rules for prose humans read: jomcgi.dev page copy, project READMEs,
`ARCHITECTURE.md` and STPA prose, demo pages, runbooks, and engineering
posts. Simple and easy to digest beats dense and complete. Higher signal
comes from cutting, not compressing.

The hard bans apply to everything written in this repo, including commit
messages and PR bodies. The voice rules apply to any prose a human will
read. The register section is about matching the reader.

## Know the reader

Two audiences, two registers.

**Public pages** (jomcgi.dev, `/ember/*`, demo pages). Visitors skim, and
many are not infrastructure engineers. Print the meaning of a mechanism;
keep the mechanics for deep docs. Translate jargon a skimming reader
bounces off ("no network card" over "no NIC"). The user-facing capability
is the hook; how it is built comes second or gets cut. Example of the
register: "the egress sidecar makes the call and attaches the real token;
the secret never enters the VM" carries the meaning without the
certificate-issuance mechanics.

**Repo docs** (READMEs, `ARCHITECTURE.md`, runbooks, ADR prose). Readers
are engineers, including future maintainers and agents. Mechanics belong
here: exact resource names, ports, verbs, file paths. The voice rules
below still apply in full.

## Hard bans (AI-tell rhetoric)

- **No em-dashes.** Anywhere. Use a comma, colon, parentheses, or split
  the sentence.
- **No "not X, but Y" antithesis** ("a design input, not a postmortem").
  State the positive claim and stop.
- **No arrow chains in prose** ("weeks -> minutes",
  "measure -> detect -> cap"). Write the words.
- **No decorative rule-of-three.** Keep a list only when every item is
  real evidence; otherwise one strong point.
- **Banned vocabulary**: "load-bearing" (total ban), "clean-sheet" (say
  "built from scratch" or name the language), "making the hard path the
  easy one", "center of gravity". If a phrase is frequently generated by
  AI tools, it is out.

## Voice

- **No connective glue.** Cut phrases that narrate a structure before
  showing it ("with a clean ownership boundary:", "which means that",
  "notably"). A semicolon split does the work; when in doubt, two blunt
  sentences.
- **No consequence narration.** After stating facts or a mechanism, stop.
  Never append a clause explaining what the facts add up to ("grant
  access once and it is correct everywhere"). The reader gets it.
- **No self-praise.** No quality adjectives about one's own work
  ("clean", "robust", "elegant"). Show the scope, the number, the
  boundary, and let it land.
- **No self-rating labels.** "Owned it end-to-end" fails "would a staff
  engineer say that?". Name the initiating act ("Found the gap") and let
  verb-led clauses carry it; the reader concludes ownership.
- **No reviewer labels.** Never introduce evidence with an editorial
  label: "the sharpest case", "most notably", "the killer example",
  "crucially". That is a critic's voice narrating significance. State
  the case and end on the fact.
- **No thesis or aphorism openers.** "Most of platform engineering is
  detection" is thought-leader material. Open with what the thing is or
  does; hang evidence off that.
- **No costume nouns.** Name the literal actor instead of dressing a
  fact in security vocabulary. "My friends have been trying to pull keys
  and exfiltrate data from it since day one. None have managed it."
  beats "The same red team has been trying since day one." The costume
  is exaggeration; the bare fact is stronger.
- **Capability over artifact.** Describe what a thing enables, not what
  was built ("building UIs" is noise).
- **Prose is sentences, not inventory.** Each sentence explains itself
  ("stateful apps and databases that sleep when idle and wake when
  called"); a paragraph or bullet past ~200 words is a wall. Prefer
  replacement over growth: new evidence pays its way by displacing
  weaker detail.
- **Reuse Joe's literal nouns.** His word over a synonym ("process",
  not "machinery"); real repo names over cute coinages.
- **Cut, don't compress.** More impact with less content means deleting
  a clause, not squeezing it into denser phrasing.
- **Headings are outcome-led and short**, never mechanism-led catalogs.
- **The best lines are short dry standalone declaratives** ("A new
  source is one proto schema."). Check every sentence against "does this
  sound like Joe?".

## Formal analysis reads as an outcome

Present formal methods by what they found, never as a credential. "An
STPA hazard analysis of the shipped code found two trust-boundary holes
I hadn't documented, both on the remediation roadmap" is the register to
copy for STPA and TLA+ material.

## Checklist before publishing

Grep the draft for the common violations:

- em-dashes, and `->` in prose
- "not just", "isn't just"
- "sharpest", "most notably", "crucially", "killer"
- "load-bearing", "center of gravity", "clean-sheet"
- "red team" where the literal actor is friends

Then read it once asking only: does every sentence sound like Joe, and
does every clause earn its place?
